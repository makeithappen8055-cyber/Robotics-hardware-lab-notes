# Robotics-hardware-lab-notes
A hands-on engineering log where I document my daily progress in robotics hardware. Tracking microcontroller builds, circuit physics, kinematics and the physics behind each component through real experiments. A transparent record of what I build, how I debug it, and why it works.

# 🔬 Robotics Hardware & Physics Engineering Journal

> *"To truly invent new hardware, one must not merely connect components—one must understand the physical laws governing them down to the electron level."*

---

## 🎯 The Moto & Vision
Robotics education often treats hardware as a black box. 
This repository is a documented effort to deconstruct, calculate, and understand every component from scratch

My objective is to systematically master robotics hardware from fundamental physics up to complex autonomous systems. While studying AI, machine learning, and mathematics in my degree, I am using this space to master the physical counterpart: the physics behind every individual component, kinematics, electronics, dynamic load mechanics, and real hardware interaction.

## 🎯Every entry here combines:

* 🛠️ **Hardware Builds & Working Projects:** Hands-on breadboarding, circuit prototypes, and working microcontroller code that I test and run directly on the bench.
* 🔍 **Mistakes, Bugs & Debugging Logs:** A clear look at what went wrong when a circuit refused to turn on, a component overheated, or code behaved erratically. I record the measurements, the root cause of the error, and the fix that resolved it.
  
* ⚛️ **Physics Notes Written in Plain English (For Future Me):** Personal notes unpacking what each part is actually doing under the hood before I wire it into a circuit.
  
* 🦾 **Sharing My Working Robots & Milestone Projects:** The complete, functioning projects and mechanisms I create as I progress. Each includes the circuit wiring, working code, and the step-by-step thinking behind how the system came together.

---
## 🚀 The Aspiration
**Immediate Goal:** Build genuine, under-the-hood intuition so I can build complete hardware projects entirely on my own, handle real-world circuit diagnostics with bench instruments, and contribute meaningfully to robotics research labs and challenges.

**Long-Term Vision:** Achieve true hardware independence—understanding the physical, electronic, and mechanical principles so deeply that I can move past following tutorials and start inventing, designing, and bringing novel robotic systems to life from my own original ideas.
